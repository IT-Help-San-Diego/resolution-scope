# DOCTRINE — "More fair than a Supreme Court judge"

**Date:** 2026-08-22 · **Stated by:** Carey · **Recorded by:** Hermes · **Repo:** resolution-scope

## The bar (Carey, verbatim intent)

> "I want this tool to be more fair than a Supreme Court judge. When they run a scan and see how
> easy it is to navigate with the keyboard and the number keys, and we line up and lock in the
> sections for the most perfect visibility and it's fine-tuned — this is going to be something very
> different. Deep breath, exhale, and the only word out of their mouth is *Haaaa. Justice.*"

The instrument's success is measured by the *felt* reaction, not the score. The verdict must land
the way a good ruling lands: inevitable, explainable, and calm.

## Why "fairness" here is mechanical, not a mood

A judge can be unfair in four ways. The instrument closes all four, *by construction* — this is
the fairness, and it is the thing we have already been building:

| A judge can… | The instrument instead… |
|---|---|
| exercise **discretion** (carrier color) | applies a **deterministic formula** — identity weights derived from severity, no per-case judgment |
| let a **mood/venue/author** color the verdict | separates **signal from carrier** — severity is a measurement, the story is labeled, never a ranking |
| issue a ruling that **cannot be re-checked** | emits a **SHA3-512 seal** — every verdict re-derivable by anyone, from the raw dispositions |
| stay **silent about the ceiling** (what the world forbade) | names it — `provider-gated`, `unmeasured`, `not-applicable` — so a gap is *attributed*, never *punished* |

"More fair than a judge" is therefore not a UX aspiration bolted on later. It is the honest-verdict
architecture (seal + truth-chain + disposition) made **felt**. The fairness already exists in the
engine; the design job is to make it *effortless and inevitable* to receive.

## The UX mandate (what "felt" requires)

1. **Keyboard + number keys are the primary navigation** — the NASA-style 1–6 section jumps already
   spec'd for the TUI. A scan should be navigable without a mouse, top to bottom, one keystroke per
   section. Effort is the enemy of the "justice" reaction.
2. **Sections locked and aligned for visibility** — fixed, predictable positions; nothing scrolls
   away; the important thing never leaves the viewport. (Same doctrine as Calibration Scope's
   "action never leaves the viewport.")
3. **Scotopic calm** — off-black, low-light, readable-by-default. A ruling lands harder when the
   room is quiet. No blinking, no marketing, no "free"-grade noise.
4. **The verdict, then the receipt.** Show the answer first, the evidence behind it, never the
   other way around. A fair ruling is a conclusion you can walk backward through.

## Where this converges with the other doctrines

- **Identity-weighting** — the score carries *importance*, the story carries *character*. The judge
  doesn't rank your choices; the instrument reports your measurement.
- **"Sane maximum"** — show perfection as far as the world lets you go, and teach the ceiling. A
  fair ruling explains the thing you couldn't have done differently.
- **"Measure the breeze, not the dress"** — probe every depth. A fair ruling doesn't judge you on a
  surface it didn't actually read.

The three-surface design (TUI / web / flipper) must carry this same felt-quality across all three:
same verdicts, same number-key navigation where the surface allows it, same scotopic calm, same
seal. "Justice" is the one word the user should breathe at the end of a scan — on the terminal, in
the browser, and through the flipper.
